# Othvek

> Nine teeth of obsidian. Eight shadows. No mouth.

## The Account of the Ash Surveyor

The surveyor entered the plain carrying a brass measure and a loaf of black bread. He had been instructed to locate the center of the ash. At the first marker, he discovered that the measure had acquired a ninth division. At the second, the bread cast the shadow of a bird.

There was no third marker. There was a stone basin where the third marker should have stood, and within it nine teeth arranged around an empty circle. The teeth were obsidian. Their shadows were bone white. Only eight shadows could be counted from any position around the basin.

The surveyor recorded this discrepancy on his sleeve. By evening, the writing had migrated to his other arm. He slept with both hands beneath his head and woke facing a direction absent from his instruments.

## A Boundary Observed From Below

The plain ends at a cliff, but only when approached backward. Walking toward it reveals more ash, another basin, and the same arrangement of teeth. The surveyor therefore crossed the last distance with his eyes fixed on the horizon behind him.

Below the cliff lay a city built from the undersides of staircases. Its inhabitants moved beneath the steps. Their lamps illuminated the ground above their heads. At the city's center stood a tower whose shadow climbed into the sky and ended in a narrow opening.

He lowered the brass measure over the edge. Its ninth division unfolded into a ladder. Somewhere below, a lamp turned toward him. The bread's bird-shaped shadow left his pack and descended first.

When he followed, the cliff became a roof, and the ash above it began to fall upward.

## The Witness With a Covered Face

The witness waited beneath the central tower. Her face was covered with a sheet of polished copper. In the copper, the surveyor saw himself standing beside the basin, still on the plain. His reflection was arranging the teeth differently.

The witness offered him a cup containing a little ash. She asked whether he had brought the missing shadow. He pointed toward the bird, now perched on the tower's inverted stair. She shook her head, and the copper showed the basin with one tooth lying flat.

He had not touched the basin. He was certain of this until a sharp edge appeared beneath his sleeve. Against his skin lay a black tooth, warm enough to suggest it had recently belonged to something alive.

The witness stepped aside. Behind her stood a doorway no wider than the tooth's shadow.

## The Shape That Held the City

The doorway opened onto a chamber lined with dark scales. Each scale reflected a staircase. At the chamber's far end, a dragon rested its jaw around the city's foundations. Its mouth was closed. Its teeth stood elsewhere, in basins across the ash plain.

The surveyor could not determine whether the creature slept. Its eye contained the image of an open eye, which contained another image, each smaller and brighter than the last. Between those images moved the city's lamps.

The witness placed the ash cup against a scale. A tiny staircase appeared inside it. On that staircase, a figure carried a brass measure and a loaf of black bread.

The surveyor put down his pack. The loaf had become ordinary stone. Its bird-shaped shadow remained outside, making no sound beneath the tower.

## An Inscription Without Orientation

The warm tooth carried marks along one edge:

*Oth — vek — ul — sha.*

He read them toward the floor. The chamber darkened. He read them toward the ceiling. The copper sheet covering the witness's face became clear, revealing another basin behind it.

Within that basin lay eight teeth and nine shadows. One shadow moved without a tooth to guide it. It crossed the empty circle, climbed the basin's rim, and rested against the surveyor's hand.

He felt a brief pressure, like someone checking whether a pulse remained. The warm tooth cooled. The dragon's jaw shifted by the width of a grain of ash, and every staircase in the city changed the number of its steps.

The witness took the tooth. She did not take its shadow.

## The Survey That Was Returned

The surveyor found himself on the plain before sunrise. His brass measure had eight divisions. His sleeve was clean. The stone loaf lay beside him, split neatly down the middle and filled with dark feathers.

He returned to the first marker and measured the distance to the basin. The result was the same from every direction: a small hollow beneath his wrist, exactly where the warm tooth had rested.

His report was delivered in a sealed wooden box. Those who opened it found no paper, only a ninth division cut from brass. Those who left it closed heard the sound of lamps being extinguished below a city.

The plain remains unsurveyed.

Nine teeth wait in the basin. Eight shadows lie beside them.

The remaining shadow has learned to travel beneath a sleeve, and no longer asks which mouth it belongs to.
